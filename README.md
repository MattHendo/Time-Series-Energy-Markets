# **Predicting Electricity Prices Using Time Series Analysis**
__By Isaac Valencia, Patrick de Beer, Cristina Ravenna, Mattias Escudero, and Matthew Henderson__

## Problem Statement and Motivation: 
In electrical markets, energy price forecasting is essential because it helps producers, suppliers, and consumers make well-informed financial and operational decisions. Market players may optimize production plans, reduce risk, and enhance bidding strategies in both day-ahead and real-time markets with the help of accurate price forecasts. Creating accurate forecasting models is still quite difficult because of the inherent volatility of energy prices, which are impacted by a number of variables, including weather, fuel prices, renewable energy sources, and changes in demand. Despite this, modern statistical analysis proves to be up to the challenge. Enabled by vast swaths of market data, we can now produce accurate results with ostensibly simple models. With that in mind, we wanted to replicate and even improve on results from industry-standard, widely used methods. Specifically, the central objective of this project is to explore whether machine learning techniques, combined with ensemble and stacking methods can improve short-term electricity price forecasting relative to individual statistical models and the official TSO forecasts. Baseline models such as seasonal naive forecasting, as well as traditional time series approaches like ARIMA and SARIMAX, are employed as first-level predictors. These individual model forecasts are then combined using XGBoost as a meta-learner, under the hypothesis that each model captures different underlying patterns in the data, and their combination can lead to more accurate and robust predictions.
